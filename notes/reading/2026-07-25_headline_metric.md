# 2026-07-25 — The headline metric, supervisory PFE, and the EPE readout (read-log)

> Session: `OQ-INT-02` and `OQ-CCR-08` resolved (`INT-23`, `CCR-RISK-03`) — the EE profile family leads the results chapter, headlined by the book-EPE delta band ($-11.0\%$ / $-6.5\%$ / $-5.6\%$ across the `INT-20` λ band); reported tail exposure is supervisory PFE $= \max(\text{quantile}, 0)$, derived at the reporting seam (`viz.with_supervisory_pfe`, `viz.epe_summary`); engine raw quantile + golden baselines untouched.

1. **Pykhtin & Zhu — *A Guide to Modeling Counterparty Credit Risk*, the whole guide (short)** (`[PykhtinZhu2007]`): the canonical role split — EE/EPE feed pricing (CVA) and capital, PFE sets limits. **Why:** this split *is* the `INT-23` ordering (EE leads, PFE secondary, CVA future work); without it the choice reads as taste rather than the standard division of labour in CCR management.
2. **Basel Framework — CRE50 (definitions), CRE53 (IMM), CRE52 (SA-CCR)** (`[BaselCRE]`): EE, EPE, Effective EPE, PFE; IMM capital EAD $= \alpha \cdot \text{EEPE}$ with $\alpha = 1.4$ (floor $1.2$) patching what EPE averages away (general wrong-way risk, granularity). **Why:** grounds "EE/EPE is the supervisory pricing-and-capital path" and the exposure $= \max(V, 0)$ convention behind `CCR-RISK-03`; the α discussion is the honest caveat that a mean-based headline hides tails by design.
3. **BCBS — *Principles for the effective management and supervision of climate-related financial risks* (June 2022) + the December 2022 climate FAQ** (`[BCBS2022Principles]`, `[BCBS2022FAQ]`): climate is a driver of *existing* risk categories — for credit, it flows into counterparty creditworthiness inside the existing framework; no Pillar-1 climate capital charge exists. **Why:** the supervisory-fit leg of `INT-23` — a climate scenario delta on the existing EE metric is exactly the prescribed integration, and confirms the session's working hypothesis (guidelines, nothing fixed).
4. **BCBS — *Climate-related financial risks: measurement methodologies* + *transmission channels* (both April 2021)** (`[BCBS2021Measurement]`, `[BCBS2021Transmission]`): scenario analysis is the quantification tool; short-horizon VaR misses decadal materialization, threshold effects, compounding. **Why:** why market VaR/ES is demoted to robustness in `INT-23` despite being hosted (`INT-06`) — the horizon-mismatch argument, citable to the supervisor itself.
5. **Gregory — *The xVA Challenge* (4th ed. 2020), the credit-exposure chapters (exposure metrics and their uses)** (`[Gregory_xVA]`): EE/PFE/EPE defined on $\max(\text{MtM}, 0)$; negative MtM is owed to the counterparty (DVA territory), not credit exposure; PFE as the limit-setting desk metric. **Why:** the `CCR-RISK-03` floor convention and the reading of PIMPA's raw quantile as a net-liability diagnostic rather than a defect.
6. **Artzner, Delbaen, Eber & Heath — *Coherent Measures of Risk*, §2–3** (`[Artzner1999]`): quantiles are not subadditive. **Why:** why the book-level "mean PE99 shift" is a summary, never a portfolio PFE — and the theory behind PFE's λ-nonlinearity on our own band (`INT-20`'s NAID-26 note), which made a quantile headline fragile across the regime scenarios.
7. **Canabarro & Duffie — *Measuring and Marking Counterparty Risk*, ch. 9 in Tilman (ed.)** (`[CanabarroDuffie2003]`): Monte-Carlo exposure measurement and marking, PFE/EE in ALM practice. **Why:** the practitioner baseline our engine reproduces; short and worth reading before writing the results-chapter framing.
8. **ECB *Guide on climate-related and environmental risks* (Nov 2020) + EBA *Guidelines on the management of ESG risks* (EBA/GL/2025/01)** (`[ECB2020Guide]`, `[EBA2025ESG]`): climate quantified inside ICAAP/credit risk; proxy and expert-judgment quantification acceptable under data scarcity. **Why:** the European template for "sophisticated internal framework" integration the thesis argues for, and cover for the estimated-parameter (proxy-grade) inputs of the jump channel.
9. **Dietz, Bowen, Dixon & Gradwell — *'Climate value at risk' of global financial assets*** (`[Dietz2016]`): climate VaR as a scenario-valuation delta, not a trading quantile. **Why:** positions our jump-on $-$ baseline readout inside the climate-VaR literature — same family, read out on exposure profiles instead of asset values.
10. **Klugman, Panjer & Willmot — *Loss Models* (5th ed.), the aggregate-loss / compound-distribution chapters** (`[Klugman2019]`): aggregate-loss quantiles as the insurance-supervisory metric family (Solvency II SCR framing). **Why:** the road not taken in `INT-23` — why the loss-distribution readout is arm-level robustness rather than the headline of a CCR-spine thesis.

Open decisions these readings feed: the sector-differentiated marks (`OQ-INT-11` — the EE band rides on them, and the wrong-way caveat sharpens the need), the scope re-baseline (`OQ-INT-08`, with CVA now explicitly future work per `OQ-CCR-04`), and the hypothesis wording residual of `OQ-INT-01`.
