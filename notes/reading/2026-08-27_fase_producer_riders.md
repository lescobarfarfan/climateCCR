# Read-log — the fase producer + trajectory-λ(t) riders (2026-08-27)

Session decision: `INT-34` (the `OQ-INT-12` (a) producer session — `pipelines/22` fragments, `pipelines/01` wiring, subset application superseding the `INT-33` per-target fail-loud, two λ(t) riders; first fase cells run). Readings ordered by priority.

1. **`[NGFS2025ST]` — NGFS short-term scenarios, technical documentation (scenario−baseline semantics, the 2025–2030 window, quarterly EIRIN vs annual CLIMACRED grain).** Why: the raw-basing ruling is a *reading* of these paths — the fragment treats the published delta as "the scenario world's deviation from the WEO baseline, tracked in calendar time", so the value already accumulated by the 2026-07-17 valuation date applies as the step-1 catch-up; whether that catch-up is economically a "reveal" (news that we are on the scenario path) or double-counts transition already priced into the observed market is exactly the interpretation caveat the documentation's baseline definition settles.
2. **`[ContTankov2004]` — Cont & Tankov, *Financial Modelling with Jump Processes*, the inhomogeneous-Poisson simulation section (time-varying intensity, per-interval expected counts).** Why: the λ(t) rider draws per-step Poisson counts with $\lambda(t_i)\,\Delta t_i$ — the step-start evaluation is the piecewise-constant-intensity discretization of an inhomogeneous Poisson process, and its adequacy rests on λ varying slowly relative to the grid (growth ≤8.1%/yr against daily-to-monthly steps in the mass-bearing window), which is the argument that lets the rider ride the tested 1-D branch without a thinning algorithm.
3. **`[BrigoMercurio2006]` — Brigo & Mercurio, *Interest Rate Models*, §3.3 (Hull–White $B(t,T)$ loading and the mean-reversion decay).** Why: the fase HWTP transition delta (−2.31% proxy vs nivel −8.43%) is smaller *partly by construction* — the in-simulation r(t) overlay moves future curves only through the model's own $B(t,T)/\alpha$ loading and carries no sovereign long anchor — and §3.3 is where that propagation (and the decay recursion the schedule inverts) lives; without it the fase-vs-nivel gap reads as a bug instead of the one-factor manuscript note materialized.
4. **`[Klugman2019]` — Klugman, Panjer & Willmot, *Loss Models* (5th ed.), the frequency-trend chapter (fitting and projecting claim-count trends).** Why: the two riders bound the arrivals-trend sensitivity exactly the way an actuarial frequency projection is stress-tested — a fitted growth applied from the current level (registry, the ceiling) against the only cross-regime-defensible growth (ciclón bridge, ≈flat) — and the `HAZ-STOCH-06` refusal to extrapolate the registry *level* is the textbook regime-break discipline.
