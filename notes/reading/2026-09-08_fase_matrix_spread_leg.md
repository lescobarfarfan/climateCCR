# Read-log — the fase matrix, the three-flavor ruling, and the spread leg (2026-09-08)

Session decisions: `INT-35` (the phased matrix + like-for-like readout, the `pipelines/23` figure set, CVA fase legs, the ruling that nivel stays the transition headline with fase as the phased robustness flavor) and `INT-36` (the valuation-side credit-spread schedule — Phase 2 of the `OQ-INT-12` arc). Readings ordered by priority.

1. **`[NGFS2025ST]` — NGFS short-term scenarios, technical documentation: the CLIMACRED variable definitions (the `corporate_bond_spread_adjustment` family, excl- vs incl-policy; the equity relative adjustments) and the scenario-vs-baseline semantics.** Why: the spread leg's family choice is a reading of these definitions — excl-policy because the fase rate leg already carries the policy delta, so the incl-policy variant would count it twice — and the DAPS_NAM full-leg fase delta (−7.37 % against −2.46 % pre-spread) is only intelligible once one sees that its transition is equity- and spread-led while its policy-rate path is economically nil; without this the spread channel's size looks arbitrary.
2. **`[Gregory_xVA]` — Gregory, *The xVA Challenge*, the CVA chapter (unilateral CVA as $\mathrm{LGD}\sum \mathrm{EE}\cdot\mathrm{DF}\cdot\mathrm{dPD}$) and the wrong-way-risk chapter.** Why: the session's sharpest finding — transition CVA *rises* under fase where it nets out under nivel — is a pure consequence of the bilinear decomposition: the credit channel (the PD path) is flavor-invariant, so a phased exposure fall that is a third of nivel's cannot offset it; the chapter also fixes why exposure and credit channels are reported separately rather than netted into one number.
3. **`[BrigoMercurio2006]` — Brigo & Mercurio, *Interest Rate Models*, §3.3 (the Hull–White $B(t,T)$ loading) and the defaultable-bond / credit-spread discounting material.** Why: two limits of the fase flavor live here — the rate leg reaches the future curve only through the model's own loading (no sovereign long anchor, `INT-33`), which is why fase sits below nivel even with the spread leg in; and the prevailing-level spread ($\max(s_0 + \Delta s(t), 0)$ on all remaining cashflows) is a flat spread term structure per date, the same object the static pricer used — the reading makes clear what a forward-integrated alternative would have changed and why it was rejected for like-for-likeness.
4. **`[FedCSA2024]` / `[BoE2022CBES]` — the Fed Climate Scenario Analysis exercise and the Bank of England CBES: how supervisory exercises apply scenario paths to spreads and prices (levels at horizons, instantaneous repricing at t=0).** Why: the headline ruling — nivel (the peak-severity t=0 state) stays the headline, fase is the phased robustness flavor — is the same convention these exercises use; the reading justifies reporting a three-flavor table instead of picking the most "realistic" path as headline.
5. **`[ContTankov2004]` — Cont & Tankov, the inhomogeneous-Poisson section (time-varying intensity, expected counts per interval).** Why: the rider results (registry −9.31 %, CT bridge −8.97 % vs headline −8.93 %) are a statement about the *time-averaged* intensity over the exposure-weighted horizon, not the terminal ×1.43; the `plot_intensity_paths` cumulative panel is exactly this reading, and the arrivals check integrates $\lambda(t)$ with the engine's step-start rule for the same reason.
