# Read-log — the numerics pin, the canonical draw, and the CENAPRED 2024 EXTENSO (2026-10-03)

Session decisions: `GEN-37` (numerics pinning policy), `CCR-SIM-02` (canonical Cholesky draw + `eigh` repair), `HAZ-CENAPRED-13` (the 2024 EXTENSO ingested), `HAZ-STOCH-07` (refits adopted), plus the verbatim re-applications of the `INT-19` and `INT-27` gates and the two-stage re-base recorded in `INT-23`. Readings ordered by priority.

1. **`[Glasserman2003]` — Glasserman, *Monte Carlo Methods in Financial Engineering*, §2.3.3 (generating multivariate normals: the Cholesky factorization, and why any square root of the covariance yields the same joint law) and §9.1 (loss probabilities and value-at-risk: the sample-quantile estimator and its asymptotic variance $p(1-p)/\big(n\,f(q_p)^2\big)$).** Why: `CCR-SIM-02` replaces one square root of the covariance (SVD) by another (Cholesky) — §2.3.3 is where the equivalence of the marginal law and the non-uniqueness of the factor are stated; §9.1 is the formula behind the tail-precision caveat (why a 99 % quantile on 10,000 paths moves ~10 % between equally valid realizations while the mean moves 0.15 %).
2. **`[GolubVanLoan2013]` (§99) — Golub & Van Loan, *Matrix Computations*, §4.2 (positive definite systems: existence and uniqueness of the Cholesky factor) and §8.6 (computing the SVD: the ordering of singular values and the sign/basis freedom of singular vectors).** Why: the mechanism of the 2026-09-24 break — the singular vectors of a near-degenerate matrix are determined only up to an ordering the algorithm resolves from rounding; the Cholesky factor has no such freedom. Without it the "stream swap" reads as a bug rather than as a property of the factorization.
3. **`[BaselCRE]` CRE53 (Effective EE / Effective EPE, EAD $= \alpha \cdot \mathrm{EEPE}$) and `[Gregory_xVA]` ch. 7 (exposure metrics: EE as a pricing object, PFE as a limit statistic).** Why: the institutional-scale reading of the "small" re-base moves — capital and limits apply multipliers and thresholds to these metrics, so a continuous 0.2 % move in an input can become a discrete outcome at a limit; and PFE-type quantiles are the metrics most exposed to the sampling error above.
4. **`[PykhtinZhu2007]` — the CVA integral and its linearity in the exposure profile.** Why: the base CVA moved +0.17 % with PDs unchanged, i.e. through the exposure channel alone; linearity is why percentages transfer to a book of any size with the same composition.
5. **`[ContTankov2004]` ch. 3 (compound Poisson processes) and `[Klugman2019]` ch. 9 (aggregate loss models).** Why: `HAZ-STOCH-07` — why the floor leg deepened on severity alone (same λ, John's revised damage) and the CT leg on arrivals alone (three extra 2024 cyclone rows); the compound-Poisson mean $\lambda\,E[L]$ is what the aggregate-loss panel's analytic check uses.
6. **`[PielkeLandsea1998]` §§2–3.** Why: the reporting-vintage lesson of `HAZ-STOCH-07` — the preliminary 2024 report understated the final total by 43 %; loss series are revised, and a living calibration (`GEN-31`) must track data vintages as a source of parameter risk.
7. **`[eng]` conda-lock documentation (https://github.com/conda/conda-lock — the lock model, multi-platform solves, `conda-lock install`) and numpy's build notes on BLAS selection (`np.show_config`).** Why: `GEN-37` — what the lockfile pins, why PyPI numpy wheels link the OS BLAS on macOS, and how the manifest's `blas` field is derived.
