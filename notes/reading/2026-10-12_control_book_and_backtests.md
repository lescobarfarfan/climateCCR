## Readings, in priority order

1. **`[Kruttli2025]` (Journal of Finance), `[DessaintMatray2017]` (§99) and `[HongLiXu2019]` (§99) — the exposed-versus-unexposed designs of the physical-risk literature.** **Why:** the control book is the portfolio analogue of their control groups: Dessaint and Matray compare firms in the hurricane's neighbourhood with unaffected firms, Kruttli, Roth Tran and Watugala sort firms by the share of establishments in the landfall region, Hong, Li and Xu sort food stocks by drought trend. Sector membership as the sorting variable is exactly what the `INT-24` S-tier matrix encodes, and `[Bua2022]` (§99, ECB WP 2677) finds that sector membership is what captures *physical* exposure in European prices while firm-level data capture *transition* exposure. The result that the control book moves by 39 % of the climate book while its $\sum w\gamma$ is 24 % of it says the exposure structure (option moneyness, desk mix) carries part of the heterogeneity, not only the sensitivities — the reading `[Bressan2024]` makes for Mexico at asset level.
2. **`[AnderssonBoltonSamama2016]` (FAJ) and `[Jondeau2021]` (BIS WP 985) — benchmark portfolios with climate tilts (§99).** **Why:** the alternative construction the plan kept as optional: start from the market index and re-spread the excluded names' weights with minimal tracking error. The adopted control book is a matched-structure portfolio rather than an index replica because the question is impact heterogeneity on a *given* trade structure (30 netting sets, the same desks and rules), and because an IPC replica overlaps the climate book by construction. Read alongside `[Battiston2017]` (climate-policy-relevant sectors as the exposure sort, Nature Climate Change) and `[Gorgen2024]` (the brown-minus-green factor) for the long–short view the control book deliberately does not take.
3. **`[Glasserman2003]` ch. 1 and `[Gregory_xVA]` on exposure as a paired difference — the Monte-Carlo yardstick.** **Why:** the two books are not paired realisations (different correlation matrices and union grids draw different paths under one seed), so the difference of BOOK shifts carries $\sqrt{\mathrm{sd}_A^2 + \mathrm{sd}_B^2}$ from the two seed studies (0.03 / 0.015 / 0.02 pp). The 5.5 pp headline gap is a 200-sigma event on that scale; the honest uncertainty of the heterogeneity statement is the λ band and the author tiers in the S matrix (`OQ-INT-13`), never Monte-Carlo noise.
4. **`[Klugman2019]` ch. 15–16 (goodness of fit, model selection) and `[Garwood1936]` — the actuarial out-of-time test.** **Why:** tier A is actual-versus-expected validation in the cat-model sense: the Poisson predictive of the test-window total, the lognormal PIT of every test loss, and the compound-Poisson predictive of the annual aggregate scored by PIT, central coverage and CRPS. The severity rejection on the ≥ 200 MDP cyclone stream (KS $p < 0.01$) is the lesson of `[PielkeLandsea1998]` in reverse: the thresholded 2016–2024 record is measured differently (report regime, `HAZ-CENAPRED-10`), so a shape test across the break is partly a measurement test. The A/E on the mean (0.74) versus the median (0.49) shows how tail-driven a $\sigma_L \approx 1.3$ lognormal mean is.
5. **`[BCBS2021Measurement]` and `[Acharya2023]` — what climate models can and cannot be backtested on.** **Why:** the BCBS measurement-methodologies paper and the GARP/CRISIL commentaries say long-horizon climate scenarios cannot be backtested on history; Acharya et al. argue for market-based validation with public data. Today's layer is the constructive answer: what *can* be backtested is the hazard/loss model on the observed record (tier A), the loss→mark seam on the insurer's realised loss ratios (tier B), the risk-factor engine (tier C), the climate component's incremental predictive content (tier D) and exposure on a static portfolio (tier E). Read with `[Giglio2021]` and `[Campiglio2023]` for where physical-risk pricing stands in the climate-finance literature.
6. **`[Klugman2019]` ch. 9 (aggregate loss) with `[Dietz2016]` — the loss-ratio reading of tier B.** **Why:** the modelled annual mark $\beta S/K$ *is* an insured loss ratio, so the CNSF paid-over-premium series (continuous 2008–2024, no publication break) is the one realised series the H2 channel can be scored on. Three readings bracket the truth: raw (A/E 1.50 — attritional claims included), bridged by the trigger share of damage (0.964, so nearly identical) and the cat-excess over the attritional floor of 0.33 (A/E 0.47). The channel's magnitude sits between a 1.5× under- and a 2× over-prediction with near-nominal coverage — the climate-VaR literature's scenario-delta reading (`[Dietz2016]`) applied to a realised loss series.
7. **`[BCBS2010Backtesting]` (§99) — *Sound practices for backtesting counterparty credit risk models*, December 2010, read in full.** **Why:** the design of tiers C and E: non-overlapping fixed-horizon forecasts aggregated over many initialisation dates (¶13), every risk-factor model that feeds EPE backtested separately at several horizons including one year (¶18–19), calibration data stopping at the initialisation date (¶24, the ¶41 back-pricing), reliability read at several percentiles, never one (¶25, ¶31), static hypothetical representative portfolios re-struck in the spirit of their first introduction (¶33–34), and the explicit statement that long-horizon results are not expected to be significant (¶38). Practitioner frameworks on top of it: `[Anfuso2017]` (§99, Risk.net) and `[Ruiz2014]` (§99, Journal of Credit Risk).
8. **`[Kupiec1995]`, `[Christoffersen1998]`, `[Berkowitz2001]`, `[DieboldGuntherTay1998]` (all §99) — the market-risk backtesting canon.** **Why:** the statistics in `risk/market/backtest.py`: proportion-of-failures and independence tests on daily VaR exceptions, the likelihood-ratio test on $\Phi^{-1}(\mathrm{PIT})$, and the probability integral transform as the common currency (iid uniform under the true conditional density). The daily VaR99 rejection for every name (median hit rate 2.6 %) is the textbook fat-tail finding; the one-week over-coverage of the body (0.66 at nominal 0.50) with tails on target is leptokurtosis seen from the other side.
9. **`[JamesWebber2000]`, `[CKLS1992]` and `[Hausman1978]` — the rate leg's identification problem, again.** **Why:** the tier-C and tier-E rate refits reproduce `MKT-CALIB-06` and `GEN-33`: the adopted F-TIIE 2006-start Vasicek is unidentified at 2019 (AR(1) $\phi > 1$) and lands a 14 % long-run level at 2022, while the TIIE-28 2001-start tie-breaker is identified and stable at every date ($a$ 0.48–0.56, level 5.7–6.5 %, $\sigma$ 1.6 %). The one-year rate coverage of 0.29 / 0.35 / 0.65 is the single-factor cone failing to span the hiking and cutting cycles, the finding the architecture already builds around.
10. **`[AmisanoGiacomini2007]`, `[DieboldMariano1995]` and `[GneitingRaftery2007]` (all §99) — comparing two density forecasts.** **Why:** tier D scores realised returns under the plain GBM and under the Merton mixture the engine actually simulates (the jump totals drawn by `ClimateJumpProcess` over one horizon step, Rao-Blackwellised through the Gaussian mixture density), and tests the log-score differential with Diebold–Mariano — the Amisano–Giacomini weighted-likelihood-ratio test in its unweighted form; CRPS is the proper-score companion.
11. **`[Merton1976]`, `[ContTankov2004]` ch. 3 and `[MacKinlay1997]` — why a rejected jump-on *price* density is the expected result.** **Why:** the adopted marks are insured loss-ratio contributions applied as log-price jumps (`INT-17`): $\lambda E[\text{mark}] = 19.29 \times 1.43\,\% \approx -28\,\%$ per year on the $\gamma$-weighted book, and ≈ −110 % per year for a $\gamma \approx 4$ name. Realised prices rejecting that density is the same statement as the pre-registered event-study nulls (`INT-18/19`, `INT-27`), now in density form; the compensated variant (drift matched, shape only) is not rejected at one year, and at one month nothing is distinguishable. Read this before writing the results chapter: the channel is a transmission overlay on exposure, not a forecast of BMV prices.
12. **`[BaselCRE]` CRE53 and `[Gregory_xVA]` — what tier E scores.** **Why:** realised netted MtM per counterparty at the reporting dates against the forecast paths, both legs, and realised $\max(V,0)$ against forecast EE — the direct EPE backtest the sound-practices paper asks for (¶29: test the whole distribution, read EE off it). The dated books are hypothetical representative portfolios: option strikes ATM at the dated spot, swap rates and bond coupons off the dated curve, maturities shifted by one offset (¶34). The rate side is model-mapped (the pricers rebuild the curve from the realised short rate through $B(t,T)$), which the 0D pin quantifies: the forecast and realised 0D values differ by at most 248 MXN on netted MtMs of order 100 k.
13. **`[Anyfantaki2025]` and `[Battiston2025CLIMACRED]` — the rate marks under dated calibration.** **Why:** the dated $S_{\text{rate,eff}}$ follows the dated $a$ through the loading $\bar B(T) = (1 - e^{-aT})/aT$, so with $a \approx 0.5$ the rate marks are ≈ 3.4–3.9× the reference medians — the `INT-22` logic (inversion and engine must share one $a$) exercised at three dates; the rate channel still contributes a few hundredths of a percent to the BOOK shift, so this is a consistency point, not a results driver.
