# Read-log — the due-diligence sweep before the manuscript (2026-10-20, second session)

Readings behind the session's decisions: the control-book S-tier band (`INT-41`), the annual tier-E grid with the realised-curve reprice and the one-factor span reading (`INT-42`, `OQ-MKT-14`), the close-out collateral pairing (`CCR-RISK-09`), the reference verification (`CCR-LIT-05`) and the landing backlog (`GEN-41/42`). Companion explanation: [[2026-10-20_due_diligence_sweep_explained]].

## Priority readings

1. **`[BCBS2010Backtesting]`** — ¶13 (non-overlapping fixed-horizon forecasts, aggregating over initialisation dates), ¶21 and ¶34 (the representative-portfolio re-strike), ¶24 (calibration data stop at the initialisation date), ¶38 (long horizons as reasonableness), ¶41 (back-pricing). **Why:** `INT-42` scores every horizon on all ten July dates *and* on each horizon's greedy non-overlapping subset — the ¶13 reading now has a column in `summary.csv` — and the realised-curve reprice removes the "model-mapped rate side" caveat ¶41 warns about; without ¶13 the annual grid looks like ten independent tests when the 2Y and 3Y windows overlap.
2. **`[NelsonSiegel1987]`** (the parsimonious curve) and `MKT-CURVE-05` in [[DECISIONS]] (the τ-profiled linear fit, the full-tenor strip). **Why:** the realised reprice strips and fits the MXN curve at every simulation date up to the data end (`yield_curve.curve_on`); the discount factors the rate legs use are $P(t,T)=\exp(-\tau\,z(\tau))$ off that fit, so the fit's long-end misfit (`MKT-CURVE-06`, 10–12 bp) is now part of the realised path, not of the model.
3. **`[LittermanScheinkman1991]`** (the three-factor decomposition of bond returns — level, slope, curvature) and **`[BrigoMercurio2006]`** §3.3 (the Hull–White bond price $P(t,T) = A\,e^{-B(t,T)\,r_t}$, $B = (1-e^{-a(T-t)})/a$) and ch. 4 (the two-factor G2++ model). **Why:** the realised-curve reading of `INT-42` — the gap between the model-mapped and the quoted-curve coverage at 2–3Y is the slope / curvature content of 2016–2025, which a one-factor model cannot represent because every tenor moves with the short rate through the fixed loading $B(\tau)/\tau$; without §3.3 the sentence "cannot span the curve" reads as a calibration complaint when it is a statement about the model's state space, and without ch. 4 the remedy named in `OQ-MKT-14` has no definition.
4. **`[Gregory_xVA]`** ch. 7 (collateral: margin period of risk, the close-out date, what the balance at the default date means) and `[BaselCRE]` CRE53. **Why:** `CCR-RISK-09` — each close-out date nets the collateral balance frozen at *its own* default date (close-out = default + MPOR), the balance a counterparty would actually hold through the MPOR; the previous loop netted the most recent default-grid balance walked past, which for the first three close-outs was the two-week one. The goldens' collateralised columns moved by the size of two weeks of balance accrual on the fixture book.
5. **`[Glasserman2003]`** ch. 2 (random streams and substreams). **Why:** `GEN-42` — the S-tier jitter draws one substream per sector *name* (`infra.get_stream_rng`, the `INT-14` pattern) so a sector's draws no longer depend on which other rows the S matrix holds; the re-drawn Mexican ordering statistic (τ median 0.893, min 0.787) differs from the 29-row stream's (0.896 / 0.768) only through the stream, the structured variants reproduce byte-for-byte.
6. **`[Bua2022]`** Table 11 (NACE Rev. 2 Level-1 section returns regressed on the text-based Physical Risk Index — mining, transport & storage and telecoms significant after 2015) and **`[ECB2021EconomyWide]`** Chart 16 (share of firms subject to high physical risk by NACE Level-1 sector; the paper's own conclusion that physical risk is location-, not sector-driven). **Why:** `INT-41` (a) — neither paper publishes a sector exposure score vector, so the fourteen control-sector S rows keep their author tiers with these two as the ordering references, and the control book's tier-contrast variants (flatten −5.91 %, sharpen −1.22 % on the headline leg) are the honest width of that judgement.
7. **`[Goldberg1991]`** (rounding and ulps) — only the "Systems Aspects" section. **Why:** the regenerated goldens of `CCR-RISK-09` were produced on the Linux machine: their uncollateralised columns differ from the macOS-generated files at $\le 10^{-13}$, inside the regression tolerance, the `GEN-39` reading applied to tracked fixtures.
8. `context/REFERENCES.md` §15 and §16 (new): the thirteen backtesting keys and the six engineering / numerics keys promoted out of §99, each line now carrying the exact venue, pages and DOI the manuscript will cite; `literature/refs_check.csv` is the resolution record.
