# 2026-08-01 · Storm-cluster sensitivity and the triage resolution — explained

**What changed (`INT-28`).** CENAPRED's registry records a named storm once per state it damaged, so the calibration counted Hurricane Alex as 3 separate "events" and Ernesto as 6. The sensitivity re-defines the event as *the storm*: same-name rows in the same year and peril are merged into one event whose damage is the sum. Applied after the ≥200 MDP-2025 trigger filter, the merge changes neither which damage enters nor its total — so the average yearly loss the jump channel injects, $\lambda \cdot \bar{L}$, is identical *by construction*. Only the packaging moves: fewer, larger events (registro trigger set 270 → 252; report-regime floor 65 → 42; ciclón bridge 229 → 164).

**What the parameters did.** Arrival rates fall exactly with the merge count ($\lambda$: 19.29 → 18.00, 7.22 → 4.67, 9.96 → 7.13). The fitted lognormal fattens ($\sigma$ registro 1.211 → 1.245; the cyclone conditional 1.332 → 1.468, mean-matched down per `INT-26`), and the *fitted* mean $\mathrm{median}\cdot e^{\sigma^2/2}$ rises even though the empirical mean per year is conserved — a Jensen wedge, mild for registro (+1.7%) and large for the floor (+77%), because the floor window is named-storm-heavy (36 of its 65 rows sit in multi-row storms).

**How to read the band.** Book EPE delta −7.85 / −3.74 / −4.63 % (headline / CT anchor / floor) vs the adopted −8.36 / −5.05 / −4.51 %. Headline and floor move within the ±1pp envelope every other sensitivity produced, so the headline claim survives the grain change. The CT anchor moves most (+1.31pp) for a transparent reason: it takes only its arrival *level* from the pure-cyclone bridge (`INT-20`), and cyclones are exactly the multi-row storms, so clustering cuts its $\lambda$ by 28% while its marks stay registry — the shift tracks $\lambda \cdot E[L]$ almost linearly. Manuscript note: the CT anchor's dominant uncertainty is the arrival *grain*, not the window. The deepest per-name supervisory-PFE99 shifts *ease* under clustering (fewer arrivals dominate fatter per-event severity at this book's scale). The adopted band is unchanged — this is a robustness row, like the S-tier variants, not a re-base.

**The triage resolution (`HAZ-CENAPRED-12`).** The 41 flagged Chiapas rows carried `danio_mdd` values whose implied peso/dólar rate (from $\mathrm{razon} = \mathrm{MDP}/\mathrm{MDD}$) sits between $1.4\times10^{-5}$ and $145{,}135$ against year medians of 9.7–13.5 — no historical FIX reproduces them, and the one-state concentration marks a capture defect, so the MDD column is discarded (it never fed a fit). The MDP side is kept where the event is in climate scope and its magnitude is justified: the two trigger-set rows are historically corroborated (Juan de Grijalva 2007, TS Larry 2003); eleven rows are out of climate scope; three carry no value; twenty-three small in-scope rows are accepted mechanically; and the two 2003 forest-fire rows are excluded from unthresholded diagnostics because the fire season is corroborated but their specific magnitudes are not — a conservative verdict that cannot move any adopted number (both are sub-threshold), reversible by moving two ids and re-running the pipeline.

**Justification.** Aggregation of a compound Poisson process is closed under merging arrival points ([ContTankov2004]); MLE severity refits on re-grained samples move the fitted moments through $\sigma$ ([Klugman2019]); the grain-vs-physics caveat follows the normalized-damages literature ([PielkeLandsea1998 ref?]).
