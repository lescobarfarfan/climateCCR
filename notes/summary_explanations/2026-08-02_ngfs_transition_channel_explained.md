# The NGFS short-term transition channel, explained — what was built, what the numbers mean, and why it is defensible

**Session:** 2026-08-02 · **Decisions:** `MKT-NGFS-04..07`, `INT-29`, `INT-30` (refining `MKT-NGFS-02/03`) · **Producers:** `pipelines/15 → 16 → 01 → 17` · **Readout:** `results/ngfs_epe_readout/book_epe_deltas.csv`

This note explains the first end-to-end climate-*scenario* channel of the thesis: NGFS short-term transition scenarios landing on the Mexican counterparty-credit book as curve shocks, read out as book-EPE deltas next to (and combined with) the HAZ-estimated physical jump channel. It is written to be self-contained: a reader who knows the project's jump channel but has never touched NGFS should come out able to defend every number in the readout table.

## 1. The machine in one paragraph

The NGFS short-term vintage (May 2025) publishes, for four narrative scenarios and a baseline, quarterly model-world paths of macro-financial variables 2025–2030. We pull two of them for Mexico — the EIRIN policy rate for Mexico's macro-region and the CLIMACRED Mexican sovereign-spread adjustment — difference the policy rate against the package's own baseline run, take each series' *peak* deviation over the scenario window, and interpolate those two numbers across the tenors of the **current** market MXN zero curve (the 2026-07-17 strip): policy-rate delta at the short end, total sovereign-yield delta at the long end. The twelve shocked pillars are written into a per-scenario copy of the book's market-data root, and the existing simulation machinery — untouched — runs the full CCR valuation on it: the Hull–White short rate now diffuses around the shocked forward curve, every IRS, option, and cebur reprices, netting and collateral apply, and the book-level EPE comes out. The difference against the unshocked run is the transition channel; running the HAZ climate jump on top gives the combined climate readout.

## 2. The decisions, in plain language

**Why short-term first (`MKT-NGFS-04`).** Two vintages exist: the long-term Phase V (annual to 2100, IAM-based) and the short-term May-2025 package (quarterly 2025–2030, three coupled models, financial variables built in). The user chose ST-first. What that buys: quarterly resolution exactly over the book's exposure ramp, *direct financial outputs* (the ST package was designed to feed stress tests — it publishes sovereign and corporate valuation adjustments, not just macro aggregates), explicit Mexico coverage in the country-grain models, and one connector that clears the Phase-1 definition of done without inventing a splice between two model worlds. What it costs: the scenario window ends in 2030 while the book grid runs to 2077 — which is why the first build uses the *fixed* (level-shock) flavor, where the window only supplies the shock size (§2, last item). The long-term vintage is deferred, not rejected; Phase VI (due 2026) triggers a re-pull under the living-calibration policy (`GEN-31`).

**Why the package's own baseline, and what "market-related" really means here (`MKT-NGFS-05`).** The shock is a difference: $\Delta r = r_{\text{scenario}} - r_{\text{baseline}}$. Differencing *within* the model suite — scenario run minus the package's IMF-WEO-Oct-2023-anchored baseline run, both produced by the same GEM-E3/EIRIN/CLIMACRED system — makes model bias and the stale WEO vintage cancel to first order, and keeps our deltas comparable to every number NGFS itself publishes (all their reported impacts are deviations from that baseline). The tempting alternative — differencing against market-implied forwards to be "market-consistent" — does the opposite of what it sounds like: NGFS paths are not market forecasts, so scenario-minus-market embeds the whole model-vs-market wedge inside the shock and double-counts whatever transition expectations the market has already priced. Market-consistency in this design comes from the **landing point**, not the baseline: the model-world $\Delta r$ is applied to the *observed* Banxico curve (`MKT-NGFS-01`), which is exactly the Fed CSA and CBES practice of scenario deltas on top of current conditions. One clean sentence for the defense: *the baseline isolates the shock; the current curve supplies the level.*

**The two anchors under short-term data grain, and how Mexico was located (`MKT-NGFS-06`).** The original two-anchor rule (`MKT-NGFS-02`) wanted the policy rate at the short end and a 10Y government yield at the long end. The ST dataset publishes no 10Y yield variable and no country-grain policy rate — but it publishes something arguably better for the long end: `sovereign_spread_adjustment_incl_policy|MEX`, an annual, **Mexico-specific**, already-baseline-differenced total adjustment of the sovereign spread including the monetary-policy transmission. So the adaptation is: short anchor = quarterly $\Delta$(EIRIN policy rate), long anchor = the CLIMACRED Mexican sovereign adjustment *including* policy. No double counting occurs because each tenor uses exactly one number — at the short end the policy rate *is* the total yield move, at the long end the incl-policy sovereign adjustment *is* the total yield move, and tenors in between interpolate linearly; the excl-policy variant is downloaded purely so the decomposition (how much of the long-end move is policy transmission vs pure spread) can be reported. The policy rate's grain is EIRIN's five macro-regions, so Mexico needs a region assignment — and rather than assume, the assignment was **identified from the database itself**, twice over: (i) the gap between the incl-policy and excl-policy Mexican sovereign adjustments — which is by construction the policy contribution CLIMACRED applied to Mexico — tracks the *North America* policy delta almost one-for-one (SWUC 2027–30: gap 0.45/1.93/1.55/0.96 pp vs NAM 0.53/2.14/1.74/1.13 pp) while South America is 3–5× off scale; (ii) Mexican GDP collapses under the North-America disaster scenario `DAPS_NAM` (−6.7 % in 2027) and is untouched (−0.0 %) under `DAPS_SAM`. Both fingerprints say the model system itself treats Mexico as North America, so using the NAM policy path is not our convenience — it is model-consistent. It remains a documented caveat (a regional response applied to a specific country), softened further by the within-model differencing: regional *level* bias cancels, only the regional *response shape* is imported, and it lands on Mexico's own curve.

**The scenario set and the channel-separation rule (`MKT-NGFS-07`, `INT-29`).** The four ST narratives split cleanly: Highway to Paris (orderly transition) and Sudden Wake-Up Call (disorderly transition) are *transition* stories; Disasters & Policy Stagnation is a *physical* story (severe disasters, no new policy); Diverging Realities is a compound of both. Our thesis already has a physical channel — the HAZ-estimated jump, calibrated on Mexican CENAPRED losses — so running an NGFS physical narrative *combined* with the HAZ jump would count physical risk twice, once model-implied and once empirically estimated. Hence the channel-separation rule: the transition pair forms the headline (and may run jump-on, because transition + HAZ-physical are different risks), `DAPS_NAM` runs **jump-off only** as a deliberate model-vs-empirics comparison (§4c), and Diverging Realities is dropped (its compound construction cannot be cleanly separated). This same logic closes the long-open NGFS↔SSP crosswalk question (`DC-XWALK-6`): there is no data-level mapping between the transition taxonomy and the SSP physical pathways, and we do not invent one — the manuscript states only the defensible commonalities (all NGFS long-term scenarios ride SSP2 socioeconomics; the ST physical narratives sample the tail of the *current* climate distribution, which is policy-independent at a 3–5y horizon) plus the fact that NGFS's own Phase-V damage function rests on a retracted study — which is precisely why this thesis estimates its physical channel from Mexican data instead of importing NGFS's.

**The fixed flavor: why a peak level-shock, and why $a$/$\sigma$ stay put (`MKT-NGFS-06`, `INT-12`).** A scenario is a *path*; the book grid is 50 years; the ST window is 5. The fixed flavor resolves this cleanly for a first build: take the **signed peak** (maximum absolute deviation, sign preserved) of each anchor over 2025–2030 — the stress-testing convention of evaluating the most adverse point, as in the CBES design — and apply it as a permanent level reshaping of today's curve. That is a *level stress*, deliberately conservative (the scenario's worst moment held for the whole horizon), and it needs no assumption about what happens after 2030. The trajectory flavor — the full quarterly path absorbed into the forward curve, held constant beyond the window — is the documented second build (`OQ-MKT-13` a). The Hull–White dynamics parameters $a$ and $\sigma$ are left untouched because the scenario statement is about the *level and shape* of rates, not about the volatility regime; shocking the curve moves where the short rate mean-reverts *to* (the model's drift tracks the shocked forward curve automatically in `direct_input` mode — there is no separate $\theta(t)$ artifact to desynchronize), while re-estimating dynamics under a hypothetical scenario would have no data to stand on. This is also the supervisory convention (scenario paths shift levels; volatilities are a separate stress axis).

## 3. What each number is

**The anchors (percentage points, peak over 2025–2030, sign preserved):**

| Scenario | Short anchor ($\Delta$ policy rate, NAM, quarterly) | Long anchor (MEX sovereign incl. policy, annual) | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| HWTP | **+1.11 pp** (peaks Q4-2028) | **+1.96 pp** (2030) | Orderly tightening: carbon pricing passes through to inflation and policy; modest sovereign repricing. |
| SWUC | **+2.31 pp** (Q3-2028) | **+3.26 pp** (2028) | Disorderly: nothing until the 2026 policy shock, then a spike — the "Climate Minsky Moment" profile. Both anchors are zero through 2025–26, exactly the procrastination narrative. |
| DAPS_NAM | **−0.11 pp** (2026) | **+3.90 pp** (2028) | The disaster signature: policy roughly flat-to-easing while the sovereign risk premium spikes — a violent steepening, the textbook post-disaster fiscal-risk profile. |

**The shock profile.** Each pillar of the 12-pillar MXN curve (1D…30Y) moves by $\Delta(\tau) = \Delta_{\text{short}}$ for $\tau \le$ ~1M, $\Delta_{\text{long}}$ for $\tau \ge$ 10Y, linearly interpolated in tenor between (then pp → decimals). Flat extrapolation beyond the anchors is deliberate humility: the data speaks at two tenor points; inventing curvature between or beyond them would be decoration. Both the simulation (RFE) and pricing HW1F curve files of the scenario overlay are shocked identically — the scenario is one world; simulating in it while discounting in another would be inconsistent.

**The base.** The unshocked jump-off book EPE is **269,655.35 MXN** — byte-identical to the `INT-23` re-based baseline, which is the seam-regression proof: adding the whole NGFS layer changed nothing on the untouched path.

**The three readout columns** (`pipelines/17`, per scenario × λ-band config):
- `transition_pct` — scenario jump-off vs base jump-off: the **pure transition channel** (identical across λ configs by construction, since λ only affects the jump leg).
- `combined_pct` — scenario jump-on vs base jump-off: **transition + physical jump together**, the full climate-conditional book.
- `jump_within_pct` — the scenario run's own jump-on vs jump-off: the **physical band re-measured under the shocked curve**, directly comparable to the unshocked band −8.40/−5.07/−4.53 % (headline/floor/CT).

## 4. The three headline findings, and how to talk about them

**(a) The transition delta is non-monotone in shock size — and that is the CCR point, not a bug.** The orderly scenario shifts the whole curve up ≈1.1–2.0 pp and the book EPE goes *down* 0.36 %; the disorderly scenario shifts it up ≈2.3–3.3 pp — strictly more in the same direction — and the book EPE goes *up* 1.50 %. A linear portfolio cannot do that: if a +1 pp shift lowers its value, +3 pp lowers it more. Exposure can, because counterparty exposure is $\max(V, 0)$ *per netting set*: sets whose mark-to-market is negative contribute zero until a large enough shock pushes them across zero, at which point they start contributing one-for-one, while sets long fixed-income sink deeper into positive-but-falling territory that eventually floors at zero. The book aggregates thirty such kinked positions (receiver/payer IRS mixes, bond-heavy debt-only counterparties, optioned equity names), and the net response to a curve shift is a sum of one-sided functions — piecewise, convex in places, and entirely capable of changing sign as the shock grows. The small orderly shock nets slightly negative (bond-value and receiver-leg losses dominate); the large disorderly shock pushes enough payer-flavored sets across zero that new exposure outweighs them. For the manuscript this is the single best exhibit for *why climate scenario analysis must be run through a counterparty-credit engine at all*: a P&L or DV01 view would have scaled linearly and gotten both the size and the sign of the disorderly case wrong. It also explains why supervisors insist on full revaluation rather than sensitivities in climate exercises.

**(b) The physical-jump band barely moves under transition-shocked curves — the two channels are separable in practice, with one caveat.** Under the headline λ, the jump's own effect is −8.40 % on the unshocked curve, −8.56 % under HWTP, −8.67 % under SWUC: the physical channel's headline conclusion is **robust to the transition state of the world** — a result that materially strengthens the thesis architecture, because it means estimating the physical channel on today's curve (as HAZ does) does not secretly depend on the transition scenario. The caveat worth one manuscript sentence: the *inner* band legs (floor and CT-anchor λ configs) drift by ±0.4 pp and swap order under shocked curves (floor −5.07 → −4.68, CT −4.53 → −5.23 under HWTP) — the different jump mixes (arrival intensity × severity pairing) interact differently with the curve level through the same exposure kink as in (a). The magnitude ordering headline ≫ floor ≈ CT is untouched; only the near-tie between the two inner legs resolves differently. Understanding that interaction before leaning on the inner legs under scenarios is logged as `OQ-MKT-13` (e).

**(c) DAPS_NAM is the model-world counterpoint to the measured Mexican null — a contrast, not a contradiction, and it argues for this thesis's design.** The NGFS physical narrative prices a +3.90 pp Mexican sovereign move (→ +4.28 % book EPE) out of a disaster sequence, via *expectations*: in the model, investors reprice sovereign risk because disasters change beliefs about fiscal capacity and future losses. The thesis's own pre-registered event studies (`INT-18/19`) measured the *realized* per-event response of Mexican sovereign yields to actual disasters 2002–2024 and found it bounded near zero (Otis included — a +23 bp blip reverting within ten business days). Both can be true because they are different objects: an ex-ante scenario repricing of beliefs versus an ex-post average response to realized events, in a market that has so far treated Mexican disasters as fiscally absorbed. Written up carefully, the pair is a *strength*: it shows exactly why this thesis estimates the physical channel from Mexican loss data (the empirical branch) while taking transition risk from scenarios (where no historical experiment exists — there has never been a Mexican carbon-price shock to measure), which is the `INT-29` channel split in one argument. It also flags honestly what would change the conclusion: if markets begin pricing Mexican climate fiscal risk the way the model assumes, the DAPS-style number is the leading indicator of what that repricing does to counterparty exposure.

## 5. Where each leg stands in the literature

The translation architecture (scenario deltas onto current market conditions, full revaluation downstream) is the Fed pilot CSA and CBES practice (`[FedCSA2024]`, `[BoE2022CBES]`); the specific move of expressing a transition scenario as interest-rate/equity risk-factor shocks is the DNB framework, peer-reviewed in `[Vermeulen2021]` — the closest published ancestor of `scenario_shock.py`. Differencing within the scenario suite and translating to a single country's financial variables is the ACPR/Banque-de-France template (`[Allen2020]`) and the ECB economy-wide exercise (`[ECB2021EconomyWide]`); the survey `[BaudinoSvoronos2021]` situates all of these as supervisory mainstream. The primary sources are the two technical documentations (`[NGFS2025ST]` for everything short-term; `[NGFS2024]` for the Phase-V world the LT join will use), plus `[NGFS2023STNote]` for why the ST framework exists and `[NGFS2025Notes]` for the damage-function retraction. Mexico-specific legitimacy: `[Roncoroni2021]` (transition scenarios on the Mexican banking + funds system, supervisory data) and `[BanxicoUNEP2020]` (the system diagnosis); Banxico's own REF climate chapters are the standing supervisory practice (`[BanxicoREF]`, edition to pin). The SSP sentence of the alignment note rests on `[Riahi2017]`; the exposure-kink mechanics of finding (a) are textbook `[Gregory_xVA]`; the scenario-delta valuation framing (what this readout *is*, versus a trading VaR) is `[Dietz2016]`; the access tooling is `[Huppmann2021]`.

## 6. Limitations, stated plainly

- **The region proxy.** Mexico's policy-rate path is North America's, at EIRIN's 5-region grain. Model-consistent (§2) and level-bias-free by differencing, but a Mexican-specific monetary reaction (Banxico moving harder than the Fed, as 2021–23 showed) is outside the model. The long anchor, being country-grain, partially compensates — the Mexico-specific information enters there.
- **The fixed flavor is a level stress.** Holding the 2028 peak for fifty years is deliberately conservative and standard, but it is not a path; the trajectory build (`OQ-MKT-13` a) will let the shock decay/evolve as published and will need an explicit post-2030 rule.
- **Annual grain on the long anchor.** The sovereign adjustment is annual; its peak is a year-level number sitting next to a quarterly short-anchor peak. Mixing grains in peak selection is honest (each series at its published resolution) but worth a footnote.
- **Model-world objects.** Every NGFS number is a model output conditioned on a narrative — the readout answers "what would this scenario do to the book," never "what will happen." The `INT-18/19` contrast (§4c) is the empirical discipline on that statement.
- **Equity leg unwired.** The scenarios also move equities and corporate spreads (CLIMACRED publishes sector-grain adjustments); this build shocks only the rate leg, so the transition deltas are, if anything, understated for equity-heavy counterparties (`OQ-MKT-13` c).
- **The retraction caveat cuts the other way here.** The Phase-V *physical* damage function's weakness does not touch this build (we use the ST *transition* machinery and our own physical channel) — but it must be said precisely, because a careless reading ("NGFS was retracted") would overreach.
