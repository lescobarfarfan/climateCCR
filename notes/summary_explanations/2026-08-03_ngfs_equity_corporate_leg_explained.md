# The NGFS ST equity/corporate leg, explained (2026-08-03)

What was built and decided on 2026-08-03 — the `OQ-MKT-13` (c) extension of the transition channel to the book's equity and cebur legs, the (e) inner-band resolution, and the (d) `[BanxicoREF]` pin — in plain language, with the reasoning and the numbers.

## 1. What the extension is

The 2026-08-02 connector moved only the rate curve, so a transition scenario repriced the IRS and bond desks through discounting but left the 26 equities and every cebur spread untouched — transition risk was understated exactly where a Mexican book holds it (a fossil NOC's debt, carbon-intensive cement/mining equities). The ST database publishes what closes the gap: CLIMACRED's 50-sector families at Mexico country grain, `equity_relative_adjustment|<sector>` (% vs BAU) and `corporate_bond_spread_adjustment{,_incl_policy}|<sector>` (pp vs BAU), annual to 2030. The pull config now takes every variable at the Mexico grain (`variables: []` — the REST filter matches exact names only, so the region *is* the scope; the PD/WACC families land alongside for the future CVA build), same run ids and version as the first build: a scope extension, not a data revision.

## 2. How the shocks enter (and why not through the jump)

Both new legs ride the existing fixed flavor — the signed peak over 2025–2030 — as **overlays on initial conditions**: each equity's S0 becomes S0·(1 + Δ%/100) in the spot file and both GBM `initial_value` columns (drift/vol, option strikes and notionals, FX untouched — an instantaneous revaluation of current values, the CBES/Fed-CSA practice); each issuer's sobretasa becomes spread + Δpp/100, floored at 0. The **excl-policy** spread variant is deliberate: the shocked curve already carries the policy + sovereign move at both anchors, so the bond leg takes only the corporate credit component — incl-policy would count the curve twice. Nothing enters the jump process or the GBM dynamics: CLIMACRED adjustments are model-implied *expectations repricing* under a narrative, while the jump marks are *empirically estimated realized-loss transmission* (`INT-17`), and summing the two would double-count both channels (`INT-29`) while averaging a counterfactual with an estimate. The jump channel's γ/c/severity blocks keep their unshocked-book values across scenarios — the physical-exposure composition must not depend on the transition state.

## 3. The numbers

Transition-only book-EPE deltas (vs base jump-off 269,655.35 MXN): **HWTP −3.78 %, SWUC −3.65 %, DAPS_NAM −13.35 %** — against the rate-only −0.36 / +1.50 / +4.28 %. The equity and spread legs dominate the curve leg and **flip the sign** of the adverse narratives: rate-only, higher rates *raised* EPE through the exposure kink; with VISTA at −21.7/−25.7 %, CEMEX·GCC at −15.5/−19.5 %, and PEMEX's sobretasa widening +959/+1,209 bp (380/430 → up to 1,639 bp), every scenario is now a net book loss. Approximate decomposition (additivity is only approximate under the kink): HWTP −3.78 ≈ −0.36 (curve) − 3.42 (equity+spread); SWUC −3.65 ≈ +1.50 − 5.15; DAPS −13.35 ≈ +4.28 − 17.63.

The `INT-30` non-monotonicity survives one level up: HWTP ≈ SWUC at the book level despite SWUC's uniformly larger shocks, because SWUC's larger rates-up gain nearly offsets its larger equity/spread losses — the netting-set kink, not shock size, still sets the book number. At name level the alternated IRS directions spread both signs: GRUMA +7.4k, FIBRA UNO +6.0k, GAP +4.2k MXN off-leg EPE under SWUC where higher rates dominate; PEÑOLES −7.2k, OMA −5.5k, FIBRAHOTEL −5.4k, PEMEX −5.3k (debt-only: curve + 1,209 bp spread) where repricing dominates; GCC's netting set nearly extinguishes (4.3k → 0.4k).

Combined cells (transition overlay × HAZ jump-on, HWTP/SWUC only per `INT-29`): −12.00/−12.01 % headline. Jump-within — the physical band re-read inside each shocked book — matches the rate-only build to ~0.02 pp (headline −8.54 vs −8.56 under HWTP): equity marks are multiplicative in log-price, so revaluing S0 rescales both legs nearly proportionally. The physical-channel conclusion is again robust to the transition state.

## 4. What the numbers mean — the metric, read correctly

The quantity every delta in this session moves is **uncollateralized expected exposure**: per netting set, $EE(t) = \mathbb{E}[\max(V_t, 0)]$ — the expected value of the bank's *claim on* the counterparty at each future date — and EPE is its time-average over the reporting grid, summed to the `BOOK` row. It is a **credit-exposure metric, not a P&L**: it answers "if this counterparty defaulted at $t$, how much would the bank stand to lose before recoveries", which is why it is the supervisory pricing-and-capital object (IMM EAD $= \alpha \cdot$ EEPE; CVA integrates EE against default probabilities — `INT-23`).

So a **negative transition delta does not mean "the bank loses money"** — it means that in that scenario the bank expects to hold *smaller claims on its counterparties*. The book is net long counterparty-asset-contingent value: long ATM calls on all 26 equities, long every cebur, IRS directions alternated. When a transition scenario revalues the underlying assets down — VISTA −22 %, cement −16 %, PEMEX debt discounted at +959 bp over 3–7 years (roughly a 25–50 % bond-value haircut on top of the curve move) — the *claims themselves shrink*, and measured exposure falls with them. That is the entire sign of the headline row, and it is composition-specific, not a law: a book net short equity optionality (or collateral-posting under CSAs) would show the opposite sign.

One exact identity makes the table self-consistent and worth stating in the manuscript: writing $t$ = transition delta and $jw$ = jump-within (both as fractions of their own denominators), the combined cell obeys $\text{combined} = t + jw\,(1+t)$ exactly — HWTP: $-3.78 + (-8.54)(0.9622) = -12.00$; SWUC: $-3.65 + (-8.68)(0.9635) = -12.01$. The two channels compose multiplicatively through the scenario book value, with no hidden residual.

## 5. Why the numbers happen — three mechanisms

**(i) Repricing scale dominates discounting scale.** The curve shock moves discount factors by roughly $\Delta r \cdot \tau$ — a 2–3 pp shift over the 2–10 y exposures at play, i.e. a 5–25 % valuation effect *that largely nets out* across alternated payer/receiver IRS. The equity and spread legs hit 15–30 % of asset value *one-sidedly* on the carbon-intensive names, and they touch the two desks (options, cebures) that carry most of the book's positive exposure. Once both legs exist, the rate leg is the small, partially self-cancelling term — which is why the rate-only build's deltas (−0.36/+1.50/+4.28 %) were an order of magnitude smaller and could carry the *wrong sign* for the adverse narratives.

**(ii) The exposure kink aggregates non-monotonically.** $\max(V,0)$ is convex: netting sets near zero flip in and out of exposure, so the book delta is not a weighted average of shock sizes but a sum of asymmetric, set-level responses. This produced the `INT-30` (a) non-monotonicity in the rate-only build, and it survives here one level up: SWUC's uniformly larger shocks produce a book delta *equal* to HWTP's (−3.65 vs −3.78 %), because SWUC's bigger rates-up kick lifts the payer-IRS sets by almost exactly what its bigger equity/spread repricing removes from the option and bond sets. **Scenario severity ordering does not survive portfolio aggregation** — a result only a netting-aware CCR readout exhibits (a linear P&L would order SWUC below HWTP mechanically), and the strongest internal argument for running climate scenarios through the exposure engine rather than through position-level sensitivities.

**(iii) DAPS_NAM is worst because nothing offsets.** Its short-rate anchor is ≈ 0 (−0.11 pp — *policy stagnation*), so there is no rates-up kick to the payer sets; and its repricing is economy-wide rather than sector-concentrated (equity −14…−42 % including services; spreads +4.7…+11.5 pp across every sector, +13.1 pp PD adjustment even for `Power Supply`). Everything the bank holds claims on falls together: −13.35 %, the largest single number the machine has produced.

## 6. Why falling exposure is not falling risk — the wrong-way reading

The credit-loss integrand is $\int EE(t)\,dPD(t)\,LGD$: exposure is only one of its two moving legs, and the scenario moves **both, in opposite directions**. The same pulled dataset now quantifies the other leg: under HWTP the `Crude Oil` sector's PD adjustment peaks at **+15.4 pp on a 12.3 pp baseline** (PD more than doubles; `Oil` +25.0 pp, cement +8.9 pp; under DAPS_NAM even `Market Services` +7.4 pp and `Power Supply` +13.1 pp). So the honest transition story on this book is: *the bank's claims on PEMEX shrink ~30–50 % while PEMEX-sector default probability more than doubles* — the product, which is what CVA prices, almost certainly **rises** even as measured exposure falls. Three consequences:

- The naive capital reading — "EEPE fell 4–13 %, so climate *lowers* CCR capital" — is exactly the artifact the manuscript must forestall. Exposure-only deltas understate (here: invert) the credit-loss story whenever exposure and default risk share a driver. This is wrong-way risk in its textbook form, now **quantified on the transition side** for the first time in this project (`INT-23` logged it qualitatively for the physical side).
- The CVA extension (`OQ-CCR-04`) stops being hypothetical: the `pd_adjustment`/`baseline_pd` families for all 50 sectors × 3 scenarios are already in `data/scenarios_mx/ngfs_short_term/climacred.csv`, pulled and vintage-pinned. The missing pieces are proxy spreads/LGD for non-listed counterparties and the integration seam — a chapter-sized, data-ready step.
- There is also a liability-side mirror we deliberately do not model: the short puts deepen (the bank *owes* more in bad states), which is DVA/collateral territory; noted for the manuscript's scope statement.

## 7. The counterpoint, equity edition — and the 2×2 the thesis now fills

DAPS_NAM embeds physical risk via expectations repricing and moves the book **−13.35 %**; the HAZ empirically-calibrated physical jump moves it **−8.40 %** (headline); and `INT-27` measured the realized per-event equity repricing of the same peril class as **≈ 0** (63 cyclone episodes × 26 names, τ = +0.198, p_boot = .94). Same direction, same order of magnitude, entirely different epistemics — the `INT-18/19` ↔ DAPS-sovereign pattern one asset class over.

The clean way to present this is the channel × method matrix the machine has now filled: **physical-empirical** = the HAZ jump (−8.40/−5.07/−4.53 band — the thesis's own estimate); **physical-model** = DAPS_NAM (−13.35 %, never combined with the jump, `INT-29`); **transition-model** = HWTP/SWUC (−3.78/−3.65 %, combinable — combined −12.0 %); **transition-empirical** = empty *by nature of the data* (there are no realized, date-localized "transition events" in Mexican history to study, and the realized-repricing nulls suggest the short-window response would be ≈ 0 there too). The methodological stance falls out of the matrix: **estimate what can be estimated, scenario what cannot, compare where both exist, and never sum a model counterfactual with an empirical estimate.** And the matrix carries the thesis's sharpest sentence: anticipatory models put the cost of these risks at −8 to −13 % of book exposure, while realized daily repricing around actual disasters is statistically zero — *markets are not pricing these risks contemporaneously*, which is precisely why both the scenario exercise and the structural loss channel are non-redundant with market prices.

## 8. The (e) resolution — there was no reordering

Computed from the artifacts, the unshocked inner legs are CT −5.074 / floor −4.527 (the `INT-23/25/26` ordering; the stored rate-only readout agrees), and under shocked curves each deepens by ~0.15 pp with ordering preserved (CT −5.22/−5.31, floor −4.68/−4.77). The `INT-30` (b) sentence "floor −5.07 → −4.68, CT −4.53 → −5.23, drift ±0.4 pp and swap order" wrote the shocked triple in headline/floor/CT order but quoted the unshocked band in headline/CT/floor order — each leg was compared against the other's baseline, manufacturing a swap out of two benign drifts. Per-NAID decomposition caps any single netting set's contribution to the floor-vs-CT gap at 0.017 pp: smooth, second-order, no λ-config × curve interaction worth further study. Canon consequence: correct `INT-30` (b) in place at digest and close `OQ-MKT-13` (e).

## 9. Caveats to carry

PEMEX rides `Crude Oil` (upstream-dominant mix); the refining alternative `Oil` is *more* adverse (+1,680 vs +959 bp under HWTP), so the reported hit is the conservative of the two defensible mappings — GEM-E3 has no integrated-oil sector. Hotels, airports, retail, and insurance ride the `Market Services` aggregate (airports `Warehousing`, the ISIC H52 home of air-transport support activities) — their transition sensitivity is likely understated, but these are precisely the names the physical channel prices (ASUR's γ ≈ 4), so the combined cell carries their risk through the estimated channel. The CFE floor (sobretasa at 0 under HWTP's −3.04 pp `Power Supply` tightening) binds on 2 trades and nowhere else. The curve's short anchor still rides the `EIRIN 1.0|North America` region proxy. And the fixed flavor itself is deliberately severe in *timing*: the signed peak of each 2025–2030 path is applied from $t=0$, front-loading shocks the scenarios spread over five years — the stress-standard most-adverse-point convention, whose time-faithful alternative is exactly the deferred trajectory flavor (`OQ-MKT-13` a).
