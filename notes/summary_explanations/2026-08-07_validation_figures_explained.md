# The model-vs-observed validation figures, explained (2026-08-07)

What the `GEN-33` figure sets show, how to read each one, what the numbers mean, and what they honestly do and do not establish. Three sets were produced: `results/figures/model_vs_observed/` (the validation layer, `pipelines/18`), `results/figures/climate_jump_real_mexican/` (the engine-grid figure set, regenerated under the adopted calibration, `pipelines/02`), and `results/figures/results_distributions/` (the cross-run comparison family, `pipelines/19`). Every figure comes from a public `viz` function on contract-shaped inputs (`INT-15`) and every run wrote a manifest (`GEN-06`).

## 1. The design idea: validation by overlay, scored by coverage

A calibrated stochastic model is a claim about the *distribution* of paths, so the honest visual test is to simulate many paths from the calibrated parameters, draw the nested central quantile bands (50/80/95 %), and lay the one realized path on top. The single scoring number is the **empirical band coverage**: the share of observed points inside the nominal 95 % envelope, annotated on every fan (`viz.band_coverage`). If the model's density is well calibrated, coverage should sit near the nominal level; a large shortfall is not a rendering problem but a statement that reality moved in ways the model's cone does not span. This is standard central-bank fan-chart discipline (`[ECB2624FanCharts ref?]`). Color semantics are fixed for the whole layer: **model output = deep green, observed reality = near-black** — deliberately distinct from the `GEN-22` baseline/climate scenario pair so a validation figure can never be misread as a scenario figure.

## 2. F-TIIE vs the fitted Vasicek (`fan_ftiie_insample`, `fan_ftiie_conditional`, `*_all`)

**What is simulated.** The P-measure Vasicek $dr = a(b - r)\,dt + \sigma\,dW$ with the adopted weekly-MLE headline parameters (`MKT-CALIB-08`): $a = 0.0758$ (half-life $\ln 2/a \approx 9.1$ y), $b = 8.55\,\%$, $\sigma = 0.81\,\%/\sqrt{\text{yr}}$, simulated by exact transitions (`VasicekFit.simulate`) on a **daily** grid — the parameters are continuous-time, so the estimation grain (weekly) and the simulation grain (daily) can legitimately differ. The observed series is daily F-TIIE converted exactly as the calibration converts it (percent → simple Act/360 at 1 day → continuous decimals, `MKT-SIE-04`).

**The measure distinction (caption rule).** This fan is the *historical/physical* view of the short rate — the fitted time-series dynamics. It is **not** the pricing model: the engine's HW1F carries $\theta(t)$ fitted to the current curve (`pipelines/08`'s fan). Keeping the two fans apart, and saying which one a caption shows, is a `GEN-33` rule precisely because conflating P and Q dynamics is the classic examiner trap.

**The results.** In-sample (cone from 2006, $r_0 = 8.39\,\%$): **coverage 54 %** of the nominal 95 % band. Conditional (cone restarted at the observed end-2022 rate $r_0 = 10.68\,\%$): **coverage 47 %**. The observed path spends 2009–2016 pinned near 3–4.5 % and 2023–2024 near 11.5 % — both excursions leave the stationary cone, whose dispersion is bounded by $\sigma/\sqrt{2a} \approx 2.1$ pp around $b$.

**How to interpret — and why this is a result, not a failure.** A one-factor Gaussian mean-reverting model with constant parameters cannot span two decades of Mexican monetary regimes; realized rates are more persistent and more regime-like than an OU with a 9-year half-life. Three consequences for the thesis: (i) it motivates the architecture — pricing rides $\theta(t)$ (which absorbs today's curve exactly) and transition scenarios enter as *exogenous* curve shocks (`MKT-NGFS-*`) rather than being asked of the P-dynamics; (ii) it disciplines interpretation of long-horizon EE profiles — the rate factor's simulated dispersion is a model quantity, not a forecast; (iii) it is honest ammunition for the limitations section, with the standard remedies (regime-switching or two-factor short-rate models) as future work. **A true out-of-sample refit is not available for this leg:** fitting only data $\le$ 2022 ends the sample mid-hiking-cycle, the AR(1) coefficient goes explosive ($\phi = 1.003$) and the exact MLE degenerates to a near-unit root ($\alpha \to 0$ with an unbounded level) — mean reversion is simply unidentified on the truncated window. The "conditional" fan is therefore the adopted fit's conditional forward density from end-2022, which is the standard conditional-density check, and the identification failure itself is worth one manuscript sentence.

**The `_all` variants** draw all 1,000 trajectories (PNG-only at 600 dpi per `GEN-22` — a vector PDF of that many lines is unusable) so the reader sees the object the bands summarize.

## 3. Equity GBM cones (`fan_equities_headline`, `fan_equities_all`, `fan_equities_holdout`)

**What is simulated.** Per-name GBM $dS/S = \mu\,dt + \sigma\,dW$ with the book's stored fits (`RFE_GBM_Calibration.csv`, crisis windows excluded per `MKT-CALIB-03`), exact lognormal paths on a daily grid from each name's first observed close (in-sample) or from the last close $\le$ 2022-12-30 (holdout), overlaid on observed Yahoo closes; the IPC index panel rides the `pipelines/07` fit. Axes are **logarithmic**: a GBM cone is exponential in time, and on a linear axis 25 years of drift crushes the observed path into an unreadable floor line (the first render of the session demonstrated exactly this); in log space the cone grows linearly and the overlay is legible everywhere.

**The results.** Headline panel coverage: VISTA 83 %, CEMEX 100 %, ASUR 100 %, AGUA 100 %, WALMEX 99 %, FEMSA 100 %, IPC 95 %; the full 26-name appendix grid is broadly similar. The **holdout refits are stable**: fitting only pre-2023 data moves each name's $(\mu, \sigma)$ modestly (e.g. CEMEX $\mu$ 0.09 → 0.04, $\sigma$ 0.34 → 0.34; WALMEX $\mu$ 0.12 → 0.16, $\sigma$ 0.25 → 0.25), and the 2023–2026 realized paths sit inside the refit cones.

**How to interpret.** Two honesty points cut opposite ways. First, *high coverage here is weak evidence*: a lognormal cone at multi-year horizons is enormous (the 95 % band spans orders of magnitude), so almost any realized path stays inside — the informative reading is the *exits*, and VISTA's 83 % marks precisely the 2020 oil-crash excursion below the band. Second, the useful positive content is **parameter stability** (holdout $\approx$ headline) and *location*: observed paths hug the upper half of several cones (WALMEX, FEMSA, IPC), i.e. realized drift outpaced the crisis-excluded MLE drift — worth remembering whenever a jump-on scenario is read against a baseline whose drift is already conservative. For the thesis narrative these figures certify that the GBM inputs feeding the CCR engine (`INT-21`) describe the data they were fit on, at the fidelity a GBM can offer; they do not certify the GBM family itself (no volatility clustering, no jumps — which is the point of adding the climate jump channel).

## 4. Jump-channel diagnostics — the physical calibration made visible

**4a. The arrival staircase (`jump_staircase`).** Observed cumulative trigger-set arrivals $N(t)$ (CENAPRED events $\ge 200$ MDP-2025, day grain, 2002–2024) drawn as a step function against, per publication regime, the fitted expectation $N(t_{\text{start}}) + \lambda\,(t - t_{\text{start}})$ with the central 95 % Poisson band; the vertical dotted line marks the 2016 registry→report break (`HAZ-CENAPRED-10`), and each regime is judged **only against its own** $\lambda$ (19.29 registry; 7.22 report), each band re-anchored at the observed count at the regime start. Reading: the report regime tracks its line tightly — the homogeneous-Poisson description of 2016–2024 is visually excellent; the registry regime shows a clear **convexity** — arrivals run below the flat $\lambda t$ line until ≈2008 and accelerate after — which is exactly the fitted $+8.1\,\%$/yr arrivals trend ($p \approx 10^{-7}$, `INT-16`) that the adopted homogeneous headline deliberately averages over. The figure therefore displays, in one frame, the regime break honesty *and* the known within-regime trend residual (`OQ-INT-03` c's trajectory-$\lambda$ to-do), with the `[PielkeLandsea1998]` exposure-vs-climate attribution caveat still attached to any growth reading.

**4b. Marked arrivals (`jump_marked_arrivals`).** The observed registry-window record (270 events; stem height = real per-event loss, log scale) above one simulated draw of the calibrated marked process (305 events from $\lambda = 19.29$, lognormal marks). This is a visual Turing test, not a fit statistic: the simulated panel reproduces the arrival density and the typical loss scale, while two visible differences are themselves informative — observed arrivals cluster in season and by named storm (multi-state same-day stacks) where the homogeneous simulation spreads uniformly, and the observed record carries one $7\times10^4$ MDP outlier the single simulated draw happens not to reach (a draw-level statement only; the lognormal tail generates such values across paths).

**4c. The QQ pair (`jump_qq_interarrival`, `jump_qq_severity`).** Inter-arrival times vs Exponential($\lambda$) quantiles and observed log-losses vs the fitted $\mathcal{N}(\ln 905.5, 1.21^2)$ quantiles, plotting positions $(i - 0.5)/n$ (`[Klugman2019]`). Reading: severity is the strong leg — the lognormal describes the trigger-set losses well through the body with mild tail heaviness; inter-arrivals show mass at zero (same-day multi-event dates — day-grain ties, plus the storm-per-state row grain whose robustness `INT-28` already quantified) and are otherwise consistent with the exponential at the fitted rate.

**4d. Daily jump-on/off paths (`jump_paths_daily`, `jump_fan_daily`).** The engine's own `ClimateJumpProcess` (peril labels, $c_{ip}$ scaling, per-peril severity — the full adopted `INT-25/26` configuration) generated on a **daily** 3-year grid and applied through the multiplicative equity overlay to the fitted GBM of the book's most cyclone-sensitive name (HOTEL, $c_{\text{ciclón}} = 11.4$): twin paths share diffusion draws, so each orange path departs its green twin only at marked events. Realized simulated intensity 19.25/yr vs 19.29 fitted — the channel does what its config says. The headline reading: the mean 3-year jump-on/off ratio for HOTEL is **0.075** — the sector–peril construction concentrates the book-level expected drag onto the most exposed names, and this figure is that concentration made visible (to be read next to the `[Bressan2024]` coarse-proxy caveat and the `INT-27` construction-evidence status of the ordering). Companion honesty note: the engine-grid (`pipelines/02`, B3 `largo`) path figures show multi-year straight chords between far tenor pillars — a *reporting-grid artifact*, not dynamics — so daily-grain figures are the manuscript convention for path illustrations, while the B3 set remains the exposure-grid diagnostic.

## 5. The cross-run distribution family (`results_distributions/`)

**5a. The EPE delta matrix (`epe_delta_matrix`).** The `INT-30/31` results table as one annotated figure: rows = NGFS scenarios, columns = transition-only, then combined and jump-within per $\lambda$ band, diverging color around zero (orange = exposure up, green = down), with the DAPS_NAM jump-on cells printed as em dashes — the `INT-29` channel-separation rule made typographically explicit. Reading: transition-only −3.78/−3.65/−13.35 %; combined ≈ −12.0 %; jump-within −8.54/−8.68 % vs the unshocked −8.40 % — the overlay-invariance argument at a glance.

**5b. The per-counterparty shift strips (`epe_shift_distribution_{mark_states,scenarios}`).** Each labelled run is a horizontal strip of per-NAID EPE shifts (%) with the BOOK total as an orange diamond; percentages are vs each run's *own contemporaneous baseline* (the `INT-23` chain convention, stated on the axis). The `mark_states` panel walks the re-base chain — uniform −11.00 %, sector-$\gamma$ −8.74 %, peril-typed −8.44 %, adopted −8.40 % — showing that the chain's book-level moves came with a *reshaped cross-section*: the uniform state drags every equity name alike, while the sector–peril states concentrate losses (tails to ≈ −77 % for hotel/airport names) and release the low-exposure names toward zero. The `scenarios` panel shows the $\lambda$ band (−8.40/−5.07/−4.53 %) and the NGFS combined legs, where the whole strip shifts left (equity S0 and spread shocks hit baselines and claims alike) and the lone *positive* outlier is the payer-IRS netting set gaining under rates-up jumps — the exposure-kink signature. Methodological note: these are distributions **across counterparties**, not across Monte-Carlo paths; path-level exposure distributions are not materialized in any stored artifact (`DC-CCR-RISK-3` stores expectations and quantiles per date), which is exactly why the aggregate-loss quantile distribution stays open in `OQ-GEN-02`.

**5c. A quiet but valuable by-product.** `pipelines/19` recomputed every BOOK number from the raw stored comparison frames and reproduced the canon chain **to the digit** (−11.00/−8.74/−8.44/−8.40; −8.40/−5.07/−4.53; −8.54/−8.68) — an independent cross-check that the archived artifacts, the `viz.epe_summary` implementation, and the canon's recorded numbers are one consistent system.

## 6. What this session does *not* establish

The coverage findings evaluate the *diffusion* inputs, not the CCR outputs — no backtest of exposure itself is possible without a realized counterparty-portfolio history. The jump diagnostics validate the calibrated process against the record it was fit on (in-sample by construction; the regime split is the honesty device). The GBM family's known deficiencies (no stochastic vol, no endogenous jumps) are the reason the climate jump exists, not findings against it. And the strips inherit every caveat of the runs they summarize — mark states archived under earlier calibration bases are comparable in percentage terms only under the own-baseline convention.
