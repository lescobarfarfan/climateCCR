# The fase producer results, explained (2026-08-27)

Session decision `INT-34` — the `OQ-INT-12` (a) producer session. This note explains what the three scenario fragments and the first two fase runs *mean*: how to read every number produced today, why the fase transition effect is smaller than the nivel one, what the path shapes say economically, and where the interpretation limits sit. Companion read-log: [[2026-08-27_fase_producer_riders]].

## What ran today

`pipelines/22` converted the NGFS short-term paths into three deterministic fragments (`results/ngfs_scheduled_shocks/{HWTP,SWUC,DAPS_NAM}.yaml`), and two full 10,000-path Mexican-book runs consumed them end-to-end: the **HWTP fase run** (`pipelines/01 --choques-programados`, headline λ) and the **registry λ(t) rider run** (`climate_jump_real_mexican_trend_registry.yaml`, no scheduled overlay). Every number below is manifested and reproducible; the informal book metric used for same-day comparisons is the mean over the B3 grid of the summed per-NAID EE ("book-EE proxy"), *not* the official `viz.epe_summary` book EPE — the like-for-like matrix readout is session (c)'s deliverable, so today's percentages are directional, not the manuscript numbers.

## The fragments: what each scenario's path actually says

A fragment is the scenario's *deviation from its own baseline as a function of calendar time*, re-expressed on the run's Act/365 clock (raw basing, user ruling today). Reading the three:

**HWTP (orderly "Highway to Paris")** — the policy-rate delta is already **+0.53 pp at the valuation date** and phases up to **+1.03 pp** by the window end; the worst equity sector is already **−13.3 %** at valuation and deepens to **−21.7 %**. Interpretation: in this narrative the transition is *underway* by mid-2026, so the fase run applies a sizable step-1 catch-up (the market reprices to "we are on the HWTP path today") and then a comparatively gentle further drift. The catch-up is the price of the raw-basing convention: it keeps the fase path level-consistent with the published scenario and with the nivel peaks, at the cost of the first step not being "phased" — an interpretation caveat to carry, since the observed 2026-07-17 market may already embed some real-world transition.

**SWUC ("Sudden Wake-Up Call")** — the policy delta at valuation is **exactly 0.00 pp**, then climbs to **+0.96 pp**; equities start within rounding of 0 and end at **−25.7 %** (worst sector). Interpretation: this is the late-abrupt narrative, and the fase flavor is the *only* one of the three application flavors that can represent its defining feature — *nothing has happened yet at the valuation date*. The nivel state front-loads SWUC's future peak into t=0, which is exactly the fiction the phased application was built to remove; expect the fase-vs-nivel gap to be widest on SWUC in the (c) matrix.

**DAPS_NAM (delayed/divergent, North-America edition)** — the rate path is economically nil (−0.06 → −0.01 pp), while equities start at **−14.0 %** and *recover* to ≈ −0.3 % by the window end. Interpretation: DAPS_NAM's transition pain is equity-led, consistent with `INT-31`'s full-leg finding (DAPS −13.35 % was the equity edition), and its published shape is a front-loaded shock that mean-reverts — a *recovery* the nivel signed-peak extraction is structurally blind to (it keeps the −14 % forever). Fase DAPS_NAM will therefore show a much milder long-horizon effect than nivel DAPS_NAM, and that difference is *information about the scenario*, not noise.

## The HWTP fase run: how to read −2.31 % against nivel's −8.43 %

On the proxy, the fase transition channel (fase jump-off vs plain jump-off) is **−2.31 %**, the nivel one **−8.43 %** — same sign, roughly a third the size. Three mechanisms drive the compression, and they should be cited together whenever the gap is discussed:

1. **Path timing.** The nivel state applies the *signed peak over 2025–2030* at every instant from t=0; the fase state applies the *prevailing* delta, which averages well below the peak over the exposure-weighted horizon (and the book's EE mass sits in the first years, where the HWTP path is still climbing). Phasing genuinely defers shock, so exposure integrated over time falls less.
2. **Policy anchor only.** In-simulation, the single-factor world can move the future curve only through its own $B(t,T)/\alpha$ loading off the short rate; the sovereign long anchor of the t=0 flavors (`sovereign_spread_adjustment_incl_policy|MEX`, the second point of the `MKT-NGFS-06` two-anchor blend) cannot be imposed at future dates (`INT-33` manuscript note). The fase rate move is therefore *model-consistent but anchor-poorer* than nivel's tilted-curve shock, and part of the −8.43 → −2.31 compression is that missing long-anchor content, not phasing.
3. **No spread leg yet.** The nivel full-leg overlays also shock cebur sobretasas; the fase state carries no credit-spread channel until Phase 2 (`OQ-INT-12` b). Fase transition deltas are best compared against nivel *rate+equity* states until then; against full-leg nivel they are biased toward zero by construction.

The right one-line summary for the manuscript: *the fase flavor answers "what does the transition cost if it arrives on the published calendar instead of all at once" — and on HWTP the answer is roughly a third of the instantaneous-peak cost, with the remainder split between genuine phasing and the single-factor/no-spread representational limits, to be quantified properly in the (c) matrix.*

## The physical channel under fase: separability holds in-simulation

Within the fase world, jump-ON vs jump-OFF moves the proxy by **−3.97 %**, against **−3.98 %** in the plain world (nivel: −4.24 %). Interpretation: the physical climate jump's marginal effect is essentially invariant to whether the transition state is applied at t=0 or scheduled inside the simulation — the `INT-31` "jump-within overlay-invariant" separability now demonstrated *in-simulation*, not just for t=0 overlays. This matters because it protects the headline physical band's interpretation (`INT-23`, −8.93/−5.44/−4.86 %): the physical channel's story does not depend on the transition-application flavor, so physical and transition results can continue to be reported as approximately additive channels.

## The 0D pin, verified — and what it means for reporting

The fase run's jump-off leg at the 0D pillar is **byte-identical** to the stored headline run's (exact float equality across all 30 NAIDs). This is the `INT-33` t=0 pin doing its job: the valuation-date book is priced off the *observed* market in every flavor, and the fase counterfactual begins at the first simulation step. Reporting consequence (now recorded in `OQ-INT-12` c): fase 0D rows carry *zero* transition delta *by construction* — comparisons start at the first post-0D pillar, and a 0D delta appearing in any future fase readout is a bug signal, not a result. Nivel states, by contrast, *do* move 0D (they revalue the book itself), so fase-vs-nivel tables must never mix the 0D row.

## The λ(t) riders: what 19.29 → 27.66 means and what −4.11 % says

The registry rider replaces the constant headline intensity $\lambda = 19.2857$/yr with $\lambda(t) = 19.2857\,e^{0.080767\,t}$, held constant after decimal 2031.0 ($t_{clip} = 4.463$, so $\lambda$ ends at 27.66/yr, ×1.434); the CT-bridge sibling grows at +1.37 %/yr to 20.50/yr. Both are **re-anchored**: the level starts at today's headline, and only the *growth* comes from the fitted trends. This is deliberate — the literal registry extrapolation ($\lambda(2026.5) \approx 78$/yr from the 2002 fit) is precisely the move `HAZ-STOCH-06` refuted (observed 2016–2024 arrivals run 7–10/yr), so the riders isolate *trend sensitivity at the current level* instead of resurrecting a refuted level. Read the pair as a bracket: the registry leg is the ceiling ("what if the within-registry growth, p≈1.4e-7 in its own window, persisted from today"), the CT bridge the honest central trend (the only series measurable across the 2016 publication break; statistically flat, p=.17).

The registry rider moved the physical channel from **−3.98 % to −4.11 %** on the proxy — about a 3 % relative deepening for a 43 % terminal intensity increase. Why so modest: the growth acts only over the first ~4.5 years (then holds), early-year marks land on exposures that partially recover through the mean-reverting rate leg and the long book runoff, and the aggregate effect scales roughly with the *time-averaged* intensity over the exposure-weighted horizon — about ×1.2, not ×1.43. Interpretation for the robustness section: the headline EPE conclusions are *insensitive to plausible arrivals trending* — even the ceiling leg moves the physical delta by ≈0.1 pp on the proxy — which converts the `HAZ-STOCH-06` regime ambiguity from a threat into a quantified footnote. The CT-bridge leg (to be run in the (c) matrix) will bound the effect from below at essentially the headline.

## Limits of today's numbers (say these out loud when citing them)

The proxy metric is not the canon's book-EPE definition — all percentages here are directional until `pipelines/17` runs the like-for-like fase matrix (session c). The fase state carries no credit-spread channel yet (Phase 2), so full-leg comparisons are premature. The raw-basing catch-up means HWTP's and DAPS_NAM's "phase-in" begins with a one-step jump of the already-accumulated delta — defensible (level-consistency with the published scenario) but not pure gradualism, and its size (+0.53 pp / −13–15 % equity) should be reported alongside any fase result. And the subset-application supersede (`INT-34` over `INT-33`'s per-target fail-loud) trades a per-portfolio typo guard for book-grain operability; the guard that remains — an overlay touching *nothing* simulated raises, plus producer-side validation against the crosswalk — is where config-bug protection now lives.
